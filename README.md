# N2 Affixes

An Anki deck for Japanese word-formation affixes at JLPT N2 level. It covers 84 productive prefixes and suffixes, 100 senses, 300 compounds, and 168 example sentences.

The deck is designed for recognition and recall in context: each affix has one recognition card and two fill-in-the-blank cards.

Deck structure:

- `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes`
- `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes::Prefixes`
- `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes::Suffixes`

Each note has three cards:

1. **Recognition**: the affix and reading on the front; all senses, 2-3 compounds per sense, usage notes, and both examples on the back.
2. **Cloze 1**: the first example with the affix blanked.
3. **Cloze 2**: the second example with the affix blanked.

Cloze backs show the answer, the full sentence, the relevant definition, all meanings of the affix, the other example, and the sentence source. Furigana is included for kanji.

Tags include `JLPT-N2`, `n2-affix`, `affix`, `type::prefix` or `type::suffix`, topic tags such as `topic::negation`, and source tags such as `source::tatoeba` or `source::original`.

## Scope

The starting point is the 2002 `日本語能力試験出題基準 改訂版` 2級 vocabulary table. 185 candidate forms were extracted from that table. The list was then cross-checked against the 語形成 chapters of `新完全マスター N2 語彙` and 120 N2 語形成 practice items.

The final list keeps 84 affixes. This is larger than the roughly 70 items that a minimal N2 affix list would contain. The larger scope was chosen for completeness and because several affixes have more than one productive sense. Each affix is still one note, so the extra coverage does not multiply the number of notes.

Inclusion criteria:

- The item is a pure prefix or suffix, not a compound verb or an inflection.
- It is productive in N2-level compounds.
- It appears in N2 vocabulary or word-formation materials, or is a clear N2-level counterpart of an item in the 2002 table.
- It can be illustrated with 2-3 common compounds and two natural sentences at N2 level or below.

Borderline cases were resolved conservatively toward the N2 core. The list below is representative, not an exhaustive judgment of every candidate in the 2002 table.

| Excluded group | Examples | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compound verbs and aspectual verb suffixes | 〜切る, 〜直す, 〜込む, 〜出す, 〜始める, 〜終わる, 〜続ける, 〜合う | The scope is pure affixes, not compound verbs. |
| Counters and measure suffixes | 〜枚, 〜本, 〜匹, 〜頭, 〜羽, 〜隻, 〜艘, 〜発, 〜泊, 〜遍, 〜歩, 〜畳, 〜号, 〜番目, 〜時間目, 〜年生, 〜丁目, 〜位, 〜名 | Counter words, not productive derivational affixes. |
| Lexical noun second elements | 〜山, 〜湖, 〜島, 〜寺, 〜店, 〜国, 〜紙, 〜車, 〜社, 〜室, 〜園, 〜海, 〜港, 〜校, 〜祭, 〜戦, 〜船, 〜団, 〜道, 〜夜, 〜色, 〜物, 〜編, 〜集, 〜史, 〜宗, 〜酒, 〜女, 〜手, 〜問, 〜持ち | Too lexical or too basic; not N2 word-formation targets. |
| Basic prefixes | 大〜, 小〜, 新〜, 古〜, 毎〜, 今〜, 昨〜, 最〜, 第〜, 両〜, 同〜, 来〜, 初〜, 全〜, 名〜, 和〜 | N3-N5 or too elementary; not N2 core. |
| Formal, literary, archaic, or low-productivity items | 〜辛い (づらい), 〜遣い, 〜殿, 〜領, 〜論, 〜庁, 〜省, 亜〜, 明くる〜, 幾〜, 我が〜, 本の〜, 炙る, 掻く, 黴, 皺 | Outside the N2 core, archaic or rare, or not affixes. |

Some included suffixes are borderline in grammar-focused lists: 〜っぽい, 〜がち, 〜気味, 〜み, 〜難い, 〜ぶり, 〜済み, and 〜沿い. They are included because they are productive suffixes in N2 word-formation materials, even when a grammar syllabus introduces them earlier or later than N2 vocabulary.

## Method

1. Parse the 2002 2級 table and extract candidate affixes.
2. Cross-check candidates against `新完全マスター N2 語彙` 語形成 pages and 120 practice items. Only the list of tested affix forms was retained; no textbook sentences or practice item text is included.
3. Curate 84 affixes and split their meanings into 100 senses.
4. Select 300 compounds, 2-3 per sense where possible. Readings and English glosses come from JMdict, with manual overrides for numbers, names, and entries that JMdict does not cover cleanly.
5. Select example sentences. Tatoeba is preferred when a sentence passes all checks. Original sentences fill gaps, and a patch file replaces corpus sentences that are too short, too long, awkward, or missing the target sense.
6. Validate every sentence: 8-20 tokens after `fugashi`/`unidic-lite` tokenization, no vocabulary or kanji above N2 according to OpenJLPT, a listed target compound, sense coverage across the two sentences, and no duplicate sentences.
7. Render Anki ruby with `scripts/furigana.py` and build the package with `genanki`.
8. Run structural QA, import the package into a throwaway Anki collection, and render cards through Anki's own engine.

The level check has a small documented allowlist in `scripts/jlpt_check.py` for common N2-or-below words that OpenJLPT omits or mislabels as N1, such as 大変, 本当, 専門, and 開発.

## Rebuilding

The deck can be rebuilt from the curated data without the original corpus or dictionary files:

```sh
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install genanki fugashi unidic-lite requests
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_deck.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/qa_deck.py
```

`build_deck.py` reads `data/affixes_source.py`, `data/compounds.csv`, and `data/sentences.csv`. It does not need the original corpus or dictionary files.

Regenerating the sentence layer is optional and requires external files that are not part of the repository:

- `/tmp/opencode/jpn.txt` for the Tatoeba Japanese-English pairs.
- `/tmp/opencode/openjlpt.sqlite` for OpenJLPT vocabulary and kanji levels.
- `/tmp/opencode/JMdict_e.gz` for JMdict lookups.

With those files in place:

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/select_sentences.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/merge_sentences.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_deck.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/qa_deck.py
```

The intermediate files `data/sentences_draft.csv` and `data/tatoeba_candidates.json` are kept locally during development but excluded from the public repository because they contain unattributed Tatoeba candidates. The final `data/sentences.csv` includes an ID for every Tatoeba sentence.

## Publishing to AnkiWeb

1. Import `N2_affixes.apkg` into the Anki desktop app.
2. Sync the collection to AnkiWeb.
3. On AnkiWeb, open the deck list and click **Share** next to `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes`.
4. Use the description stored in the package. It is generated from `DECK_DESCRIPTION` in `scripts/build_deck.py` and includes the CC BY-SA 4.0 grant plus the Tatoeba, JMdict, and OpenJLPT attributions.
5. Submit the shared deck. AnkiWeb may hold a newly shared or updated deck for a copyright check before it appears publicly.

To update the shared deck later, share the same deck name and location again. Download counts and ratings are preserved, but existing users do not receive updates automatically. They need to download and re-import the deck.

AnkiWeb's default Shared Deck License is personal use only. The deck description explicitly grants CC BY-SA 4.0 rights, which is necessary because the compound glosses come from JMdict under CC BY-SA 4.0. The scripts remain MIT.

## Updating the deck

The deck uses stable note GUIDs derived from affix IDs, stable deck IDs, and a stable note type. Re-importing a new build updates existing notes instead of creating duplicates, as long as the deck name and note type structure stay the same.

Your learning history is preserved. Anki matches notes by GUID and keeps the existing cards, so due dates, intervals, ease, reps, lapses, and review log entries stay attached. The import updates note content only; it does not reset card scheduling.

1. Edit the data.
2. Bump the version, rebuild, and run QA:

   ```sh
   .venv/bin/python scripts/release.py 1.1.0 --note "Added 3 new suffixes."
   ```

   Or:

   ```sh
   make release VERSION=1.1.0 NOTE="Added 3 new suffixes."
   ```

   This writes `VERSION`, prepends a `CHANGELOG.md` entry, rebuilds `N2_affixes.apkg`, and runs `scripts/qa_deck.py`.
3. Import the new package into Anki over the existing deck. Do not delete the deck first. Matching GUIDs update existing notes and keep your scheduling and review history.
4. Sync to AnkiWeb.
5. On AnkiWeb, choose **Share** again next to the same deck name. The shared deck updates in place; download counts and ratings are preserved.
6. Commit and push the repository.

Users do not receive updates automatically. They must download the deck again and re-import it. Anki updates matching notes and adds new ones. Removed notes are not deleted automatically, so avoid deleting or renaming affix IDs unless you intend to leave an old note behind.

Keep these stable:

- Deck name: `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes`.
- Note type ID `1749202600` and its seven fields.
- The three card templates.
- Affix IDs in `data/affixes_source.py`.

Changing the deck name makes AnkiWeb treat the upload as a new shared deck. Changing the note type fields or templates can prevent clean updates.

### Migrating from the old deck name

Earlier builds used `Japanese::JLPT N2::Affixes`. If you already imported one, rename the existing deck before importing this package:

1. In Anki, right-click `Japanese::JLPT N2::Affixes` and choose **Rename**.
2. Rename it to `Japanese_JLPT_N2_Affixes`. Anki renames the `Prefixes` and `Suffixes` subdecks too.
3. Delete the now-empty `Japanese` and `Japanese::JLPT N2` parent decks if you want.
4. Import `N2_affixes.apkg`. Notes update by GUID, and your cards stay in the renamed decks with their scheduling and review history.

If you import before renaming, existing cards stay in the old decks and empty new decks appear. You can then move the cards to the new decks with **Change Deck**, which also keeps their scheduling.

## Known limitations

- The JLPT has not published official vocabulary or kanji lists since 2010. N2 membership is based on the 2002 table plus current N2 materials, so borderline items are a judgment call.
- Furigana is generated automatically. Context-dependent readings can occasionally differ from the most natural reading in a sentence.
- Some corpus sentences contain names or proper nouns. Those that were too short, too long, awkward, or level-inappropriate were replaced with original sentences.
- The cloze cards use normal Anki templates with a blanked target, not native Anki cloze-note cards.
- English definitions are concise learning glosses, not full dictionary entries.
